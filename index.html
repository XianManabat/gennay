My journey, stage by stage

Four stages of my life so far, from before I was born to the person I am today.

Prenatal development & infancy · Conception to 2 years

My beginning

I was born in 2007 at Diosdado Macapagal Hospital in Sta. Rita, Pampanga. My mother's pregnancy was at risk. She was bleeding about three weeks before she gave birth to me through normal delivery.

My dad was my main hands-on caregiver, since my mom started working when I was 3 months old, so my attachment grew with him. My family remembers that I walked and sang “Bahay Kubo” before I turned one, that they felt anxious whenever I cried because no one could calm me down, and that I loved babbling to other people.

  • A pregnancy that was at risk, followed by a normal delivery
  • Walking before my first birthday
  • Singing “Bahay Kubo” before age 1
  • A strong attachment to my dad, my main caregiver
Early & middle childhood

My childhood

One of my earliest memories is from age 3. I had pooped in the living room while my brother and I were home alone, and his solution was to pour a whole gallon of mineral water all over the floor.

My dad helped me learn to talk clearly, read and write by the time I was 5. School did not start well, because I was still learning about friendship. I remember pulling a classmate's hair after they stole my bag. But school also taught me what is right and wrong. Outside of school, I played with my cousins: we did pretend play, climbed trees and crawled through rice fields. I grew up as an extrovert who loves talking and socializing, and that is where my confidence and independence began. My biggest challenge back then was pretending to sleep at noon.

  • Age 3: my earliest memory
  • Age 5: talking clearly, reading and writing with my dad's help
  • Starting school and learning rules, right and wrong
  • Pretend play, tree climbing and rice-field adventures with my cousins
Adolescence

My teenage years

I grew taller than my classmates when puberty began. Emotionally, I felt like the same Alex as when I was young, because I still loved playing outside. My friendships widened as I explored more, and I stayed close to the people I felt comfortable with. At home, nothing changed: my family was still my safe and happy place, and I always asked for their permission and opinion when deciding on parts of my life.

In high school I became competitive and always strived to be at the top of my class, and I graduated with high honors. This stage also brought hard moments. I lost my grandmother during the COVID-19 pandemic, and I had my heart broken in grade 12. Those trials made me strong and pushed me to keep going.

  • A growth spurt during puberty
  • Wider friendships, with family as my constant
  • A competitive streak and graduating with high honors
  • Losing my grandmother and a heartbreak in grade 12
My present self · Emerging adulthood

Who I am now

Late adolescence shaped who I am, but emerging adulthood fits me best today. I have passed my major and minor courses in college while working, and I think being a working student deserves recognition too. Working made me more independent, because I manage tasks and decide on my own. I also manage my own money, and the responsibility of spending wisely is still hard.

I found new college friends I can rely on, and my family stays the same: they keep inspiring me to be a better version of myself. I love baking and watching movies and series to redirect my stress. My biggest challenges are managing my time and the responsibilities of being a working student. To cope, I find a coffee shop to stay in and take a full day to rest. In the future, I want to be mentally, financially and physically healthy, and to use my knowledge of psychology in a company or from home.

  • Passing my major and minor courses
  • Working while studying, and managing my own money
  • New friends in college and a steady family
  • Baking and movies to redirect stress

Theories behind my story

Three developmental theories help me understand my own experiences. Open one to read more.

Erik Erikson: Psychosocial Theory
  • The theory: Erikson proposed that we pass through eight stages of development across the lifespan. At each stage we face a social challenge, or crisis, and how we handle it shapes our sense of self (Spielman et al., 2020).
  • My experience: My dad was my main caregiver as a baby and my attachment to him grew. In school I strived to be at the top of my class and graduated with high honors. In college I found new friends I can rely on.
  • How it explains my development: In infancy, the task is trust versus mistrust, which depends on caregivers meeting a baby's needs. My dad's steady care fits that picture of building trust. In the school years, industry versus inferiority is about growing confident in one's abilities, which matches my drive to do well in class. In early adulthood, intimacy versus isolation is about forming close relationships, which reflects the friendships I am building in college.
  • Key terms: Trust vs. mistrust · Industry vs. inferiority · Intimacy vs. isolation
Jean Piaget: Cognitive Development
  • The theory: Piaget said children think differently from adults and move through four stages of cognitive development. They build mental frameworks called schemata, which they adjust through assimilation and accommodation (Spielman et al., 2020).
  • My experience: I was walking and singing before I turned one. As a child I loved pretend play with my cousins, and by age 5 I could talk clearly, read and write with my dad's help. Starting school taught me right from wrong. Today I think things through carefully and consider how a decision will affect me and the people around me.
  • How it explains my development: In the sensorimotor stage (birth to about 2), babies learn through movement and the senses, like my early walking. In the preoperational stage (about 2 to 7), language and pretend play grow, like my games with my cousins and learning to read and write. In the concrete operational stage (about 7 to 11), children reason logically about real situations and rules, like my understanding of right and wrong at school. In the formal operational stage (about 11 onward), thinking becomes abstract, like weighing consequences before I decide. This is the theory that helped me understand myself best, because it shows how my thinking grew from play to planning.
  • Key terms: Sensorimotor · Preoperational · Concrete operational · Formal operational · Assimilation and accommodation
Albert Bandura: Social Cognitive Theory
  • The theory: Bandura emphasized that people learn by observing others and that our beliefs about ourselves matter. His idea of self-efficacy is the belief that you can carry out the actions needed to reach a goal, and it grows through mastery experiences, when we succeed at something hard (Bandura, 1977).
  • My experience: My dad taught me to talk, read and write, and my family's opinions guide my decisions. I got through losing my grandmother and a heartbreak in grade 12, and today I handle work and school at the same time.
  • How it explains my development: Learning from my dad and family fits observational learning, which means learning from role models. Each hardship I have worked through adds to my self-efficacy, my belief that I can handle what comes next. That belief connects to my motto, “Take a risk, or lose the chance,” and to how I describe myself as courageous.
  • Key terms: Observational learning · Self-efficacy · Mastery experiences

Reflecting on the person I am becoming

The milestone that shaped me

The experiences that shaped me most were the hard ones: losing my grandmother during COVID-19 and my heartbreak in grade 12. They made me strong and pushed me to go on with life.

Who and what influenced me

Family and school influenced my development the most. My family guides my decisions and keeps inspiring me, and school gave me my first lessons in rules, friendship and drive.

How I have changed

Physically: I have long wanted to look slim, because as a child I felt my body was bigger than other kids my age.

Cognitively: I think things through before I decide, including how it will affect me and the people around me.

Emotionally: I have become more emotional than before, maybe because I am always tired.

Socially: I still communicate well with people, but I am more careful about which secrets and stories I share.

What I have learned

I learned how my development grew and how it affects who I am today. Piaget's theory helped me most, because it showed how my thinking grew from play to careful planning.

Who I hope to become

I want to be someone who values and loves herself, so I can give that same love to the people I love. I also want to travel the world without fear, and to be mentally, financially and physically healthy.
